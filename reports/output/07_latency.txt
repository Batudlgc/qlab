================================================================================================================
    lat_0: -0.3098 +/- 0.2637   <- not distinguishable from zero
    lat_2: +0.1507 +/- 0.6663   <- not distinguishable from zero
    lat_5: -0.1565 +/- 0.3066   <- not distinguishable from zero

   lat_15: -0.1028 +/- 0.4100   <- not distinguishable from zero
   lat_20: -0.0385 +/- 0.3051   <- not distinguishable from zero
   lat_30: -0.0656 +/- 0.3121   <- not distinguishable from zero
   lat_40: -0.5240 +/- 0.3842   <- not distinguishable from zero

  No. Across-seed dispersion swamps the differences. The only entry that
  even approaches significance is the slowest maker, and at ~1.4 sd it
  would not survive a serious test. Reporting a monotonic 'cost of
  latency' from these numbers would be reading a trend into noise.

  For reference: lag drift is sigma*sqrt(L), which reaches the quoted
  half-spread of 0.1 at L = 25 steps. The flow discontinuity
  does not sit at that value in either field, which is further evidence
  the effect is relative rather than absolute.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What this establishes: latency's cost is competitive, not intrinsic.
What it does not: any per-step price for latency. That needs a venue
model with a fixed reference population, not a self-referential field.
